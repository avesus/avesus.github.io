Turing, Von Neumann, Boole, and Faggin
June 30, 2021

Modern computing follows a remarkable intellectual and industrial line: Boolean algebra, sequential logic, integrated circuits, VLSI, the Intel 4004, Verilog, and field-programmable gates. Brian Greenforest uses that lineage to restore the physical design choices hidden behind software and AI.

Federico Faggin’s career and book Silicon make the human scale of that transformation vivid.

From Boolean Relations to Programmable Silicon

George Boole formalized the algebra. Samuel Caldwell, David Huffman, Edward McCluskey, Edward Moore, George Mealy, Akira Nakashima, and Claude Shannon connected logic with switching, state, and time.

Federico Faggin helped create the Intel 4004 and pioneered silicon-gate technology at the dawn of VLSI. Ross Freeman and Xilinx later made configurable logic a commercial medium, letting designers place new digital structures inside manufactured chips.

Keep Human Intelligence at the Center

Turing and von Neumann framed foundational models of computation; statisticians later used immense arrays of the resulting chips for machine learning. The hardware still reflects human theories, designs, data choices, and purposes.

Read Faggin’s account, connect the lineage to Verilog, and design the next machine with full awareness of the people and physical ideas that made computation possible.
