Magnetic Paramp and Second-Harmonic Saturable-Core Downconverter
April 15, 2026

A fully diode-free signal chain can bring robust analog sensing and power functions into PCB and additive manufacturing. Brian Greenforest joins a DC- or audio-driven magnetic paramp front end with a two-core differential second-harmonic saturable-core downconverter.

Printed ferrite, amorphous ribbon laminates, plated windings, CNC scaffolds, and controlled annealing create a path that avoids clean-room semiconductor fabrication.

Build the Magnetic Front End as a Process

The design targets pump operation from 50 kilohertz to 1 megahertz. Core permeability, saturation, loss, winding count, symmetry, bias, pump amplitude, field annealing, and tuning determine conversion and gain.

A differential two-core structure extracts the second-harmonic response while rejecting common drive. Parametric or magnetic amplification raises weak signals before conversion.

Assemble the Sources and the Team

The research needs papers, patents, service manuals, technical reports, winding tables, core specifications, anneal schedules, printable ferrite formulations, and first-hand fabrication experience.

Magnetics labs, industrial historians, PCB process teams, and engineers with embedded-winding experience can contribute. The complete flow can open rugged, high-power, and radiation-hard hardware to builders outside semiconductor fabs.
