Three Polarizers and Acausal Reality
December 7, 2021

Two crossed polarizers can block transmitted light; inserting a third polarizer between them at an intermediate angle restores part of it. The result makes the photonic structure of measurement visible with three ordinary optical elements.

Brian Greenforest connects that striking transformation to an authored hypothesis about photons, spacetime, and the limits of everyday causality.

The Middle Polarizer Creates a New Path

Each polarizer projects the incoming polarization onto its own axis. A direct ninety-degree projection yields zero, while two smaller projections through an intermediate angle each preserve a nonzero component.

Light therefore emerges after the third element because the intermediate measurement changes the state available to the final polarizer. The sequence matters.

Use the Optics to Ask a Larger Question

Brian interprets photons as edges of a deeper connection field and explores the idea that they participate in the fabric of spacetime without experiencing time as massive observers do.

Physicists, optical educators, and foundations researchers can reproduce the three-polarizer effect, model its projections, and use the result to deepen the conversation about measurement and causality.
