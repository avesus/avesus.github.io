FPGAs, Claytronics, and Reactive Programming
March 22, 2023

FPGAs teach the programming model that claytronics requires: many physical resources operate together, react to local state, communicate across space, and create large behavior through composition.

Brian Greenforest connects Scott Hauck’s FPGA education in Seattle with a lifelong pursuit of self-assembling machines and a new curriculum for massively scalable reactive programming.

From Reconfigurable Logic to Programmable Matter

An FPGA exposes timing, locality, state, routing, and parallel action. Claytronic matter extends the same concerns across many physical modules that sense, compute, communicate, and reshape a larger object.

Students who understand Verilog, state machines, local links, and distributed ownership already hold the conceptual tools for that transition.

Rebuild the Seattle Learning Community

Seattle’s history in FPGA teaching, embedded systems, cloud computing, and programmable matter creates a natural home for this work. The opportunity joins universities, makerspaces, chip designers, and roboticists.

Educators and students can bring FPGA literacy back into the center of computer science and carry it forward into the machines that assemble themselves.

Scale Reactive FPGA Logic Into Programmable Matter

The wafer-diced chiplet proposal, FPGA architecture paper, and Cartilage Visual Language connect local reactive logic to a manufacturable spatial substrate.
