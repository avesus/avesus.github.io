The most important mystery in intelligence is not a stream of generated language. It is the human capacity to form world models, connect meaning, choose possibilities, and live through the consequences.

Brian Greenforest places people and thought at the center of the AI conversation, drawing together Erik Larson, Sean Carroll, Carlo Rovelli, Bernardo Kastrup, and Federico Faggin.

Models Give Intelligence Its Meaning

People reach beyond predicting the next symbol. They construct internal worlds, revise beliefs, imagine alternatives, and coordinate action through shared meaning.

Questions from many-worlds physics, relational interpretation, idealism, and consciousness widen the engineering frame. They ask what an observer is and why a model matters to the life using it.

Invest in the Human Inquiry

Larson’s work challenges the assumption that more computation alone yields general intelligence. Brian’s reading connects that critique to a broader account of reasoning, experience, and possibility.

Read across computer science, physics, and philosophy. Then build systems that strengthen people’s ability to think, decide, and create rather than treating people as incidental users of a prediction engine.
