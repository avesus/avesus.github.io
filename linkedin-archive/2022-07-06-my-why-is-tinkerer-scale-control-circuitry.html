Tinkerer-Scale Control Circuitry
July 6, 2022

Communities should be able to create sophisticated control circuitry locally, revise it immediately, and keep building without waiting for a distant chip or PCB supply chain. Brian Greenforest’s goal brings VLSI-scale capability into the hands of tinkerers.

The resulting platform would support sensing, switching, gain, restoration, timing, and actuation without depending entirely on CPUs, GPUs, microcontrollers, or even conventional FPGAs.

Shorten the Physical Iteration Loop

Today a complex control design often leaves the workbench for board layout, fabrication, packaging, shipping, and assembly. Each turn stretches the gap between an idea and the next physical measurement.

A locally buildable active-circuit medium can collapse that delay. Builders could alter structure, test behavior, and learn from the machine while the question still feels immediate.

Start a Movement Around Local Capability

Fast iteration creates more than convenience. It gives schools, repair communities, laboratories, and independent inventors direct control over the circuitry that shapes their tools.

Materials researchers, makers, educators, and circuit designers can help define the process. Build the element, the interconnect, or the teaching path that lets a local community create its own machines.
