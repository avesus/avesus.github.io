Cartilage, Transputers, and CellMatrix
May 5, 2022

Cartilage carries the strongest ideas of Transputers and CellMatrix into a new post-processor fabric. Micro-FPGAs, local communication, analog one-wire SerDes links, and physical packaging let computation grow by adding more connected material.

The architecture joins power distribution, signaling, configuration, and program structure so scale becomes a property of the fabric instead of a custom cluster project.

Build on the Right Lineage

Inmos Transputers made communication part of the processor model. Nicholas Macias’s CellMatrix explored locally connected reconfigurable cells. Ross Freeman’s FPGA made programmable hardware commercially real.

Cartilage combines those lineages with small reconfigurable nodes and a runtime that manages regions, routes, objects, and ports across a physically expandable array.

Create the Platform After Processors

The opportunity reaches from data-center compute to programmable matter and locally organized machines. The fabric can accept new nodes without redesigning a fixed-size server around every expansion.

Early cofounders, investors, FPGA engineers, and distributed-systems builders can shape this platform now. Help turn the architecture, runtime, and packaging into the next computing industry.
