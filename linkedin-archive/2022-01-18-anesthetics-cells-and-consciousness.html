Anesthetics, Cells, and Consciousness
January 18, 2022

Anesthesia reaches deep into living machinery, not only into a brain-level abstraction. Research connected here reports that propofol impairs kinesin processivity, linking a change in consciousness to molecular transport inside cells.

Brian Greenforest develops that mechanism into a larger biological hypothesis: individual cells participate in sentience, while the brain coordinates the immense cellular ensemble that forms a person.

From Molecular Motors to the Whole Organism

Kinesin motors carry cargo along microtubules and support the active internal organization of cells. A compound that changes their movement offers a concrete bridge between molecular action, cellular behavior, and organism-level loss of consciousness.

The cellular lens expands the question beyond neurons alone. It asks how anesthesia affects many living cell types and how their coordinated activity contributes to the body’s unified experience.

Study Consciousness Across Scales

The resulting research program connects cell biology, intracellular transport, neural coordination, hierarchy, and participation. It treats the body as an organized society of living units whose relationships matter.

Cell biologists, anesthesiologists, and consciousness researchers can test this connection across organisms and scales. Follow the molecular mechanism, compare cell responses, and build a richer account of sentient life.
