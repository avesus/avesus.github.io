What Cartilage Is and Is Not
April 27, 2022

Cartilage concentrates on one consequential goal: replace the Turing-von Neumann programming bottleneck with a scalable machine for reactive, distributed computation. Ordinary silicon, local reconfiguration, and proven SerDes links supply the physical foundation.

The architecture moves operating-system responsibilities into hardware, removes call stacks and threads from the application model, and lets programmers reason directly about objects, state, ports, and dataflow.

The Final Chip Unifies General Computation

A network-on-chip links locally reconfigurable regions into one expandable fabric. The Cartilage runtime manages placement, routes, ownership, and object creation so application structure remains readable as hardware grows.

Specialized GPU, TPU, neuromorphic, and signal-processing blocks can attach where they add value. Cartilage carries the broad body of software that otherwise sprawls across processors, operating systems, and orchestration layers.

Build the Post-Processor Platform

The same machine opens a path toward programmable matter, local silicon production, and new physical computing systems. Its immediate value starts with a new chip architecture and the language that makes it usable.

Engineers, investors, researchers, and product partners can join the work before the platform reaches mass deployment. Bring a hard scaling problem, help build the runtime, or fund the path to silicon.
