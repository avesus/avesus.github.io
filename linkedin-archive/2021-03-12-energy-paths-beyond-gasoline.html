Energy Paths Beyond Gasoline
March 12, 2021

Replacing gasoline and natural gas requires an energy system, not a single favorite technology. Brian Greenforest connects hydrogen, efficient turbines, fuel cells, geothermal heat, thorium molten-salt reactors, fusion, and solar fuel production into a portfolio aimed at real transportation and heating economics.

Each path contributes where its physical and commercial strengths matter most.

Match Each Source to the Job

Hydrogen can carry energy into vehicles and industrial equipment. Efficient turbines and fuel cells can turn that fuel into continuous power. Geothermal systems can deliver steady local heat, while thorium molten-salt and fusion systems pursue dense generation.

Solar gains a strategic role when it makes transportable fuel. Large ocean-based generation near Hawaii could split water, compress or liquefy hydrogen, and ship energy to the machines that need it.

Build Production and Distribution Together

Generation alone cannot replace gasoline. The full product needs water processing, storage, compression or liquefaction, transport, dispensing, conversion hardware, maintenance, and competitive lifecycle cost.

Energy companies, island infrastructure partners, turbine builders, fuel-cell teams, and investors can assemble that chain. Solve production and distribution together, and the market can move.
