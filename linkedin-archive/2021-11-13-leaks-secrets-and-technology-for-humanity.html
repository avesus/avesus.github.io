Technology Secrecy and Manufacturing Concentration
November 13, 2021

Political and military secrets and technical capability serve different public purposes. Brian Greenforest argues for protecting genuine national-security information while opening the engineering knowledge that lets humanity build, repair, manufacture, and respond to planetary challenges.

Technology grows through structured access, education, and reuse, and open-source businesses show that openness can generate durable commercial value.

Organize the Body of Human Technical Knowledge

Process recipes, device physics, equipment design, software, metrology, manufacturing practice, and maintenance knowledge become more valuable when people can find, understand, and improve them.

Concentrating essential semiconductor capability in a handful of institutions creates fragility. Open technical education can grow the talent and infrastructure that a resilient manufacturing network needs.

Build Profitable Systems Around Openness

Red Hat demonstrated that companies can sell support, integration, certification, and trusted delivery around open source. Hardware companies can create similar value through tools, services, quality systems, fabrication, and product execution.

Technology leaders can separate legitimate operational confidentiality from blanket technical secrecy. Open the knowledge that expands human capability and compete through the excellence of what the organization delivers.
