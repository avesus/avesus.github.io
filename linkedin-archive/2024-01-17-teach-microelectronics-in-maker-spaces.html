Teach Microelectronics in Maker Spaces
January 17, 2024

Microelectronics belongs in maker spaces. Children and adults should learn how active devices, lithography, deposition, etching, measurement, packaging, and circuit design work by building micrometer-scale chips and PCBs locally.

Brian Greenforest proposes that every American town grow semiconductor talent through direct physical practice.

Bring the Semiconductor Process Into Reach

A community laboratory can teach masks, photoresist, exposure, development, etching, deposition, oxidation, microscopy, probing, and failure analysis at geometries that visible equipment can handle.

Students can connect each process step with transistor behavior, digital logic, PCB interconnect, and the product that uses the circuit.

Grow Capability Instead of Importing Dependency

University research remains essential, and maker-space education can widen the path into that expertise. A larger domestic talent base strengthens manufacturing, repair, security, and invention across many industries.

Schools, towns, universities, equipment donors, and semiconductor professionals can build the curriculum and laboratory. Bring STEM home and let every community make active circuitry.
