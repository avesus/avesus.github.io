Chip Capacity as DDoS-Style Distribution
December 8, 2021

Internet infrastructure handles concentrated attack by distributing capacity across geography. Semiconductor supply can use the same architectural move: many local mature-node fabs operating inside one coordinated knowledge network.

Brian Greenforest proposes a 250-nanometer CMOS capability in every city with more than 50,000 people.

Distribute Fulfillment, Coordinate Knowledge

Local fabs can supply control electronics, industrial systems, education, repairs, vehicles, infrastructure, and products that do not need leading-edge geometries. Geographic repetition gives the network capacity and resilience.

TSMC can lead the consortium that carries process knowledge, training, recipes, equipment standards, quality systems, and continuous improvement into those sites. Central expertise and local output reinforce one another.

Turn Public Investment Into Manufacturing Reach

Subsidizing a few giant facilities preserves concentration. A distributed program can instead create regional jobs, practical semiconductor education, shorter supply lines, and a much larger base of physical production.

Cities, manufacturers, universities, equipment suppliers, and semiconductor leaders can build the first network. Establish one repeatable mature-node line and make it a pattern that every region can adopt.
