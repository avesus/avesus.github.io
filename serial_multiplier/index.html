One Product Bit Per Clock With No Gap Between Words

July 26, 2025 · Updated July 28, 2026

This multiplier never drains between operand words.

The 8x8 Logisim circuit accepts positive operands LSB-first. After a fixed three-clock pipeline latency, it emits the low eight product bits at one bit per clock while the next operand enters immediately.

A Compute Graph Needed Multiplication In Stream Form

Brian invented the circuit on July 26, 2025, when a standard Verilog * slowed timing closure and scarce hard multiplier blocks constrained the intended compute graph. The graph needed a streamable arithmetic path instead of another wide immediate product.

This design keeps arithmetic narrow, regular, and bit-serial. Every word advances on one cadence, with consecutive operands touching at their boundaries.

The project contains reusable var, add, and mul circuits plus harnesses. var serializes each operand; add holds carry state; mul builds and reduces the partial-product streams.

The Schedule Stays Full Across Every Word Boundary

Inputs and outputs travel LSB-first. The rendered 8-bit circuit has three clocks of fixed latency, matching the depth of its balanced serial reduction tree. After fill, a product bit leaves on every clock.

Bubbles-free means exactly this: operand word two begins immediately after operand word one, and result word two follows result word one without a drain interval.

The stream uses power-on phase rather than per-word reset/start framing or ready/valid backpressure. Its surrounding circuit identifies the bit-0 clock and maintains the cadence.

Three Reusable Circuits Form The Datapath

The schematic combines three compact roles:

  • var serializes an 8-bit value into a one-bit stream.
  • add is the clocked serial adder and carries the serial carry state.
  • mul generates partial-product lanes and reduces them through a balanced tree of serial adders.
  • dbg, main, and main2 are inspection and harness support.

Delayed and gated bit streams carry partial products directly into the reduction tree. Clocked serial adders retain carry locally, replacing a wide parallel matrix and its long carry chain.

Width Adds Lanes Instead Of A Wider Multiplier Block

Resources scale linearly with input width. Wider versions repeat lanes and reduction structure; each doubling of operand width adds one clock to the balanced reduction tree.

The rendered circuit maps 8x8 positive inputs to the low eight product bits. Extending the output width preserves the same continuous schedule.

The Architecture Matches A Streaming Compute Graph

Uniform scheduling, narrow local links, and graph-style composition replace a wide immediate product. Multiplication follows the same bit-stream rhythm as the surrounding arithmetic.

Hard multiplier blocks and inferred * operators optimize other design points. This circuit creates a spatially regular alternative for graphs that value cadence, locality, and composability.
